Recently I found myself in a position where I did everything I should do as I planned at the start of the week, but I still didn't feel the "I was productive this whole week" dopamine, and I started to think about it.

This thinking process led me to separate the things I plan as "I need to do those actions". I always put the Have To things (working for money, studying) first in the to do list, as everyone should (of course, have to's are deeply personal things). Then come the necessary ones, the things where it is not the end of the world if I don't do them for some reason, like chores. And I always find myself with a full schedule for the week, since I am studying and have a part time job, and since it is a characteristic of mine not to settle for low effort on anything, so I try to be more productive whenever I have the chance. This type of scheduling led me to being overwhelmed without even realising it. I always believed that if I can be productive at all times there is no need to overthink or to have stress (and there is no time to have stress either, not just "I did everything I could" relaxation lol). But overworking over a long period showed high stress effects on my body, and I had to accept what I did not want to accept until now. I am stressed. So I found a solution (that is the first thing I have to do when I see a problem, because of my prioritisation): adding a third type of work, wellbeing work (ww).

Calling wellbeing work a discovery probably sounds awful, but hear me out. I know it is just relaxing a bit, that watching movies or any kind of entertainment does the job, and that we already have a name for it: having free time. But there is a catch with "having free time". Personally, I never put a slot on my schedule called "free time", so whenever I have spare time for myself I just keep studying, or do something coding related like building a website from scratch (like right now, haha). And even when I do decide to have free time, it makes me feel guilty for not doing anything productive. That is why I call it wellbeing work.

Basically, ww is self care, watching series, and sometimes travelling across Europe, which needs a fair amount of budget. Its priority is third for now. It could be second, but I am starting from third since I had no self time at all before. And I know that scheduling free time sounds robotic, and that it is impossible to go a week without scrolling on your phone or watching something. What I mean is closer to a line from _Green Book_: "Whatever you do, do it 100%. When you work, work. When you laugh, laugh. When you eat, eat like it's your last meal." You need to acknowledge that the free time slot is there for one thing only: to entertain yourself and to take care of yourself. I have done this for two weeks and the only change so far is that I stopped feeling guilty on Sundays.

But doesn't this turn rest into another thing to fail at? Yeah, probably. I have not figured that one out yet. Ask me in a month.
